Where HMR hooks into science

A fair question: you want me to believe in God, so where is the science? Science explains very well what happened after the Big Bang. HMR adds a step-by-step account of how a non-physical intelligence became a physical universe, with no step that breaks logic.

Philosophers of science call the links that tie a theory to established science bridge principles. The step-by-step account is HMR's bridge: the exact place where it hooks into physics.

Claims tied to tests

HMR's most tangible part is a model of the healthy body, built from published measurements and tested by simulation. Its first test is stated so that it can fail.

Hypothesis

The densification hypothesis

In a physics-based model of the ideal body, making the fascia denser and tighter in chosen places, while leaving the rest ideal, will by tension alone change the whole body's shape, leave other regions slacker, and strain what runs through the tissue. The prediction is that for the right places and amounts, these effects will match the measurable signs of real diseases.

How it can fail

The hypothesis fails wherever densification produces only local effects, or wherever the resulting shapes and measurements do not match those of people who actually live with the disease. We will report those results as plainly as any success.

It is a hypothesis to be tested by simulation. It is not a medical claim, a diagnosis, or a treatment.

East and West, checked against the research

HMR holds that the world's body practices were working on the fascia web, and that East and West are two languages for one reality. Here is what the research measured, and what is only proposed.

The field is careful. One 2026 review frames these links as analogies rather than proven equivalences (Liu and others, 2026).

Measured

Meridians follow fascial planes

Mapping the arm, researchers found an 80% correspondence between acupuncture points and planes of connective tissue (Langevin and Yandow, 2002).

Measured

Lines of fascia carry force

A systematic review of dissection studies found strong evidence for three long myofascial chains (Wilke and others, 2016).

Measured

Meridians and collagen bands

Along one meridian, lower electrical impedance went with bands of collagen under the skin. Two other meridians showed no difference (Ahn and others, 2010). A result worth following, not yet a rule.

Proposed

Qi as fascial activity

Researchers have proposed that the activity of fascia is what the classics called qi (Finando and Finando, 2012).

Open

Chakras and fascial junctions

We found no rigorous study of whether the chakras match places where layers of fascia meet. It is one HMR aims to test.

Open

What the long-hold studies do not show

The human studies measure the whole muscle and tendon, and partly stretch tolerance. None isolates fascia. Long-term change in human fascia from long holds is still a hypothesis.

How we looked for others

We searched our own library of 183,279 items (as of 23 November 2026), the open web, and OpenAlex, an open index of more than 250 million scholarly works.

In OpenAlex, 184 works pair fascia or connective tissue with tensegrity. Only 18 of those also touch acupuncture, meridians, yoga, qigong, or consciousness, and none that we read ties them to one intelligence behind reality.

HMR's claim of novelty is narrow: four ideas held together, which are fascia as the body's main structure, fascia as a tensegrity, East and West as one reality, and one Universal Intelligence behind it all. Many researchers hold one or two. We have found no one who holds all four and builds practices and tests from them. If we missed someone, tell us.
